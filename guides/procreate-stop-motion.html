Stop motion in Procreate.

Guide · iPad · Weeks 2 and 3 · Animation Assist

A note on language. Classical stop motion means moving a physical object a tiny bit, taking a photo, moving it again, and so on for hours. We will do some of that in Week 3 with clay and whiteboards. The Procreate workflow on this page is a sibling discipline: frame-by-frame drawing with onion skinning, which gives the same chunky, hand-made feel without the physical setup. It is the fastest way to get something animating in your hands on day one.

Watch the three videos in order. The first is a tool tour, ten minutes, no project. The second is a build-along, you make a small looping animation start to finish. The third is for after you have made something and want better vocabulary to talk about it in crit.

What to bring to class

  • iPad with Procreate installed and updated to the most recent version.
  • An Apple Pencil if you have one. A finger is fine for the first week.
  • One concrete idea for a 4 to 8 second loop. Not "something cool." A specific small thing: a tea bag dunking, a foot tapping, a face cycling through three expressions.

Common first-week problems and the fix

  • Animation Assist is not showing up. Open the wrench, tap Canvas, toggle Animation Assist on.
  • Frames feel jittery and wrong. You probably have onion skinning turned off, or your timing is set too fast. Try 8 to 12 frames per second to start.
  • Lines drift between frames. That is normal for hand-drawn. Lean into it, or pick a couple of fixed registration points (eyes, a horizon line) and re-anchor every frame.
  • The file is huge and the iPad is hot. Drop the canvas resolution. You do not need 4K for a 5-second loop.

What to do next

Once you have a loop you like, export it as an MP4 or animated GIF from the wrench menu and drop it in our shared class folder. We will look at a handful together in class. You do not need to be ready to defend it. You only need to have made it.
