Exploring digital art making.

Guide · Browser tools · Weeks 1 through 5, exposure phase

A lot of artists you would recognize build small systems instead of single drawings. Code decides where the lines go, a slider changes the mood, a mode swaps the whole rule set, and the artist's job becomes choosing and tuning rather than drawing every mark by hand. This page is a set of places to go poke at that idea yourself, no install, no account, no cost. Open a few, waste twenty minutes, keep whatever surprises you.

Everything below runs in a browser tab on the machine you already have. Nothing here needs a login to try, though a few will ask if you want to save your work.

Code as a brush

Text input, sliders, and modes instead of a pencil. These are small versions of the same idea: build the system, then let it make the art.

Community · p5.js & Processing

OpenProcessing

Thousands of generative sketches, most of them remixable in the browser right on the page. Find one you like, open the code, change a number, watch what breaks. The fastest way to see how many different visual worlds come out of the same handful of rules.

Browse sketches › Editor · Write your own code

p5.js Web Editor

The official browser home of p5.js, the friendliest creative-coding language there is. Start from a blank sketch or one of the built-in examples, and you are drawing with loops and math in under five minutes.

Open the editor › Live coding · Video synth

Hydra

A modular video synthesizer you patch together by typing, built for people with no coding background. Change a line, the visuals update instantly. Feels less like programming and more like turning knobs on a machine that happens to be made of text.

Start patching › Zero learning curve · Mouse or touch

Weave Silk

Move your mouse or finger and watch symmetrical, silk-like trails bloom behind it in real time. No settings to learn, no code to read. Good for the first five minutes of any class period when you want the room quiet and looking at something pretty.

Draw with silk › Keyboard · Sound and animation

Patatap

Every letter key on your keyboard triggers a sound and a burst of animation. It is closer to an instrument than an art tool, and it is exactly the kind of playful system this page is about: press a key, something visual and musical happens at once.

Play the keyboard ›

Google's playground

Google's experiments site is a running archive of small browser projects built by artists and engineers together. Worth a slow scroll on your own time, but here are a few that fit this class directly.

Hub · Dozens of pieces

Arts & Culture Experiments

The full archive, filterable by medium and by what museum or artist partnered on it. Silk (above) started life here. Set aside real time for this one, it is a rabbit hole in the good sense.

Explore the archive › Audio-visual · Playful

Chrome Music Lab

Built for kids, genuinely fun for adults. Rhythm, melody, and sound get turned into shapes and colors you click and drag, and every experiment inside doubles as a tiny lesson in how sound and visuals can drive each other.

Open the lab › AI-assisted · Drawing

AutoDraw

You sketch something rough, and it guesses what you meant and offers clean icon versions to swap in. A fast, low-stakes way to talk about where the human ends and the assist begins, which is a live question for anything we make the rest of the semester.

Try a sketch › Game · 20 seconds a round

Quick, Draw!

Draw a prompt in twenty seconds while a neural net guesses what it is, live, stroke by stroke. Less a tool than a demonstration, but a genuinely fun one, and it makes machine pattern-recognition feel concrete instead of abstract.

Draw against the clock ›

Free stop motion, no software to buy

Procreate covers the frame-by-frame drawn version of stop motion (see the other guide). These three cover the physical-object and sketch-to-3D versions, straight in a browser tab.

Camera capture · Photos, clay, toys

Brush Ninja

Shoot frames with your webcam or phone camera straight into the browser, reorder them, and export a loop. Built for exactly the classic stop-motion setup, an object, a light, and a lot of small nudges, minus the app install.

Start shooting › Sketch to 3D · Research tool

Monster Mash

Draw a flat character, watch it inflate into a 3D shape, then animate it by dragging its limbs around, all without ever touching a 3D viewport. Built by researchers at Google and Disney and given away free. The closest thing on this page to a magic trick.

Sketch a monster › Classroom-built · Any device

Cloud Stop Motion

Designed for exactly our setup, a shared classroom device or a Chromebook, saving to the cloud instead of local storage. A solid backup if Brush Ninja's camera access is fighting with a particular laptop.

Open the tool ›

Photo effects in the browser

For the weeks we are pushing on a still image rather than motion. All three are free tiers with no install.

Full editor · Layers, masks, filters

Photopea

A Photoshop-grade editor that runs entirely in the browser and opens PSD files directly. If you already know Photoshop from anywhere, the menus will feel familiar in about thirty seconds. Free with ads, no account required.

Open an image › Filters · Overlays · Effects

Pixlr

A gentler entry point than Photopea, built around one-click filters, overlays, and effect stacks. Good for fast, loud, playful transformations rather than precise editing.

Apply an effect › One job · Instant

remove.bg

Drop in a photo, the background disappears in a couple of seconds. Small, single-purpose, and genuinely useful prep work for collage, compositing, or anything you plan to drop into another scene later in the semester.

Cut a background ›

Playful text animators

Letters as the material instead of a photo or a blank canvas. All three run entirely in your browser, no account, and you can copy out real code when you are done if that matters for the piece.

Sliders · Variable fonts

Font Playground

Type your own text, then drag sliders and watch the letterforms stretch, thin, and warp in real time. Variable fonts are a single file that can morph between wildly different weights and widths, and this is the fastest way to feel what that means.

Type and drag › Five axes · One free typeface

Recursive

A single variable typeface built around five sliders: monospace, casual, weight, slant, and a cursive switch. Type a sentence, then push every slider to its extreme and watch the same letters go from rigid code font to loose handwriting.

Warp some letters › 30-plus effects · Exports real CSS

Animated Text Generator

Type text, pick a mode (bounce, glitch, typewriter, wave, and dozens more), tune the timing, and it hands you working CSS and HTML. Nothing leaves your browser to make it happen. Good for a title card on a video piece later this semester.

Pick a mode ›

Animate in Adobe Express

Berklee gives every student and staff member an Adobe account, and Adobe Express is the fastest tool in that suite for adding real motion to a design. It is not code-based like the tools above, but it is the most direct route from "flat image" to "moving piece" if that is the direction you want to take an assignment. Two short official tutorials, then the reference page for when you forget a step later.

START HERE · Beginner series · Animation and video

Adobe Express Animation & Video, tutorial for beginners

Straight from Adobe's own channel. Covers bringing a static design to life and moving into short video, the two directions most people want when they first open the animation panel.

NEXT · Focused walkthrough · Text animation

How to animate text in Adobe Express

Narrower and faster than the first video. If all you need is a title or a caption that moves, this is the shortest path there, In, Out, and Looping animation types explained one at a time.

Reference page for later, when you know what you want and just need the exact menu: Animate your designs in Adobe Express, Adobe's own help documentation.

How to use this page

  • Pick two or three, not all eighteen. Depth on a couple beats a skim across everything.
  • Screenshot or screen-record anything that surprises you. Half of what makes these tools worth discussing in class is what you did not expect them to do.
  • None of this needs to look finished. The point of exposure weeks is trying tools on, not shipping a piece.
