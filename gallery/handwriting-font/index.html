How to fill in the boxes

Each box has four guide lines. Here they are with a few letters written across them:

  • Capitals line (top, dotted). Capitals, numbers and tall letters like b, d, h, k, l reach up to it.
  • Small-letters line (dashed). The top of a, c, e, m, x and other short letters.
  • Baseline (solid). Every letter sits on it.
  • Tails line (bottom, dotted). The tails of g, j, p, q and y reach down to it.

The lines are guides, not limits. Write the way you normally do; only the edge of the box is a hard limit.

Examples, one per box

Korean, Japanese and Chinese boxes

These boxes have a dotted square instead of lines. Write each character so it fills the square, the way you would on manuscript paper.

  • Korean: write each consonant and vowel (ㄱ, ㅏ, ㅘ…) on its own, full size. The app shrinks and arranges them into all 11,172 syllables, so 한, 글 and 값 come out without you writing them.
  • Chinese: the boxes have a rice-grid (米字格) guide. The pages run in frequency order, so the most useful characters come first, and a font you compile partway through already covers the most common ones. Read each page as you finish it; your progress is saved.
  • Japanese: write each kana once. Write the ゛ and ゜ marks where they would sit on が or ぱ, in the top-right corner. The app adds them to the right kana and builds small kana like ゃ and ッ for you.

The PDF includes this guide as its first page.

More tips for clean letters
  • Use a black felt-tip or gel pen. Pencil is usually too faint.
  • Stay inside the box and keep the black corner squares clean.
  • Print at 100% or “fit to page” on Letter or A4. Both work.

Installing on iPhone or iPad
  1. Tap Download for iPhone and iPad and save the .zip to Files.
  2. In the Files app, tap the .zip. It unpacks into a .mobileconfig file.
  3. Tap the .mobileconfig file. iOS says “Profile Downloaded”.
  4. Open Settings, tap Profile Downloaded near the top (or General → VPN & Device Management), then tap Install.
  5. Your font now appears in apps that let you pick fonts, like Pages, Keynote, Notes documents and many drawing apps.

iOS will label the profile “Not Signed”. That’s expected for a profile you made yourself. To update the font later, install the new file; it replaces the old one with the same name. To remove it, go to General → VPN & Device Management.

Installing on Android

Android has no profile file like iPhone. The .ttf font file is what you install. How far it reaches depends on your phone.

  1. Tap the .ttf download button and save the file to your Downloads folder.
  2. In apps that accept your own fonts (this works on every Android phone): open the app’s font or text menu, choose to add or import a font, and pick the .ttf. Many drawing, photo-text and design apps offer this, such as Phonto and ibis Paint X.
  3. Across the whole phone (menus, messages, notifications): stock Android, including Pixel phones, doesn’t allow custom system fonts. On Samsung, Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo and Realme phones, the free app zFont 3 can apply a .ttf: open its Local tab, pick your file and follow its steps.

zFont 3 is made by an independent developer, not Google or your phone’s maker. Recent Samsung updates tightened font rules, and zFont’s workaround for One UI 8 and later is still experimental, so back up your phone first. Characters your font doesn’t include show in the phone’s normal font.

Backup

Every page you read is saved in this browser as you go. Browsers can clear saved data, so keep a backup file too, especially on a long project. A backup also moves your work to another device.
