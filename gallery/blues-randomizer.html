Blues Randomizer, the midterm as it will actually run.

Web Audio + CSS 3D · Browser · 2026 · Kramer Gibson

What happens

  • Press Roll. Three dice spin and land on a key, a tempo, and a feel. That is your call.
  • Press Play. Two bars of stick count-in, then a band comes in and loops the 12-bar blues under you.
  • The lit cell in the twelve-cell strip is the bar the band is on, and it names the chord. Quick change in bar 2, turnaround on the five in bar 12.
  • Sound on. The bass line and the drums are the whole point, and there is no click to hide behind.

Why it exists

On the midterm I pick a key, a tempo, and a drum feel at random, and you play the blues over it. That is a nervous thing to walk into if the first time you see it is the day it counts. So here it is, the same dice, the same six feels, the same eight keys, running in a browser from now until the midterm. Roll it as many times as you want. The point is that no combination on the day is a combination you have never met.

Play along with it. When one key keeps ambushing you, that is the useful information, and it is better to find it in September than in the room.

Practicing with it

  • Open the KEYS & FEELS tab on the right to narrow the pool. Start with F, G, and C and add keys as they stop scaring you.
  • The tempo slider under the middle die goes wherever you need. Take a hard key slow first, then walk it up.
  • Bass and Drums mute independently. Kill the bass and you have to hold the form yourself, which is the real test.
  • Change key, tempo, or feel while it is playing. The band follows without stopping.
  • The dice can be set by hand: click a die, or use the arrows under it. Handy when you want to drill one thing.

Tools used

  • Web Audio API for the whole band, synthesized live: kick, snare, hats, ride, cross-stick, and four bass voices, no samples
  • A lookahead scheduler timed by the audio clock, so the groove does not drift the way a plain timer does
  • Each feel carries its own rhythmic grid, which is why the swing and 12/8 feels are genuinely in triplets rather than straightened
  • CSS 3D transforms for the dice, which reshape to however many options are switched on
  • No libraries, no build step, one file
