Flipbook Lab, an animation studio that runs in your browser.

Canvas + Web APIs · Browser · 2026 · Kramer Gibson

Open Flipbook Lab ›

Opens full screen in a new tab. Works on a laptop, an iPad with a Pencil, or a phone.

What it is

I was not happy with the free stop-motion tools online, so I built one. It does three kinds of animation in the same timeline: draw frame by frame with an onion skin, capture stop motion from your camera with the last shot ghosted over the live picture, or draw one object and move it with keyframes so the in-between frames are filled in for you.

Start here

  • Press New and pick the bouncing ball starter. Press Play. Then click the diamonds on the Ball row and drag the sliders on the right. That is keyframing.
  • Press New again, blank this time. Draw something, press N for a new frame, draw it again a little different against the red ghost. Ten frames of that is a second of animation.
  • Press Camera, put anything on your desk, capture, move it, capture. Twelve captures is one second.
  • Press Help for every shortcut. Space plays, comma and period step, N is a new frame, D duplicates.

Habits, not features

  • Save a Version before a risky idea. Then try it. Then compare. This is the whole assignment.
  • Duplicate and nudge instead of redrawing. Hold a frame for two instead of drawing it twice.
  • Watch the loop twenty times. The note you need is on loop twelve.
  • Press Spark when you are stuck. It hands you a constraint.

Your files

Nothing you make is uploaded anywhere. The page autosaves in your browser, and Save downloads a project file (name.flipbook.json) that you can open again on any computer. Export gives you a GIF or a WebM video to hand in, or every frame as PNGs if you want to finish somewhere else.

Tools used

  • HTML canvas for every layer and the compositing, pointer events for pen pressure and touch
  • MediaRecorder for video export, getUserMedia for the camera, IndexedDB for autosave
  • One small library (gif.js) fetched only when you export a GIF
  • One file, no build step
