LoopGrid, a video looper for your Mac.

Mac app · Camera + microphone · 2026 · Kramer Gibson

Download LoopGrid 0.4 ›

It runs on macOS 14 Sonoma or newer, on Apple silicon or Intel, and the file is about 1 MB.

What it is

Press the spacebar and LoopGrid records a video of you with its sound. Press it again and that take starts looping right away. Record another and it locks in time with the first. Each loop gets its own tile, and the grid grows as you add more, up to nine. It works like a looper pedal, except every layer is also a video of you playing it.

Install it

  1. Download the zip file and double-click it to unpack the LoopGrid app.
  2. Drag LoopGrid into your Applications folder.
  3. Double-click LoopGrid to open it. Your Mac will say it can't check the app for malicious software. That's because this is a free class tool, not one sold through Apple. Click Done.
  4. In System Settings, open Privacy & Security and scroll down to the line that says LoopGrid was blocked. Click Open Anyway and enter your password.
  5. Open LoopGrid again. Allow the camera and the microphone when it asks.

You only do steps 3 and 4 once. On macOS 14 you can instead right-click the app, choose Open, and click Open in the box that appears.

Start here

  • Wear headphones. Then the loops don't feed back into the microphone.
  • Press Space to record. Clap, sing, or tap a beat. Press Space again to close the loop.
  • Press Space again to record the next layer over the top. It snaps to the length of the first loop.
  • The number keys mute a loop. Delete removes the newest one, and Z brings it back.
  • Press G to stack every loop in one picture, like onion skin in animation.
  • Press E for a cartoon look on a loop: a black-and-white painting, a comic, or an ink drawing.
  • Hold X for a stutter. Press B to play a loop backwards, with the picture reversed too.
  • Press R to record the whole performance as a video file in your Movies folder.

Every key is on the bar at the bottom of the window, and the ? key opens the full guide. You can also read the guide here.

Lining up picture and sound

Webcams send the picture a little after the sound. Record a loop of yourself clapping, then press the [ and ] keys until each clap looks and sounds like one moment. Your iPhone can be the camera too, through Continuity Camera. Pick it in Settings, which opens with Command and comma.

Your files

LoopGrid never goes online. Your loops live in memory while it runs, and the only files it writes are the performance videos you record with R.

Tools used

  • Swift and SwiftUI for the app
  • AVFoundation for the camera and AVAudioEngine for sound, with every loop timed by the audio clock
  • Metal on the graphics chip for the grid, the overlay and the cartoon effects
