Blues Orbit, the minor blues on a wheel.

Canvas + Web Audio · Browser · 2026 · Kramer Gibson

How to watch

  • Click once to launch. Sound on, this piece is half music.
  • The wheel has twelve sectors, one per bar. The pointer at the top never moves; the wheel turns one click per bar, like a game-show wheel passing its peg, and the sector under the pointer is the chord the band is playing right now.
  • This is the minor blues: i and iv for the first eight bars, then the home stretch. Bar 9 is the ♭VI, bar 10 is the V7, and bar 12 is split into two half-sectors, iiø7 then V7♭9, two chords sharing one bar to whip you back to the top.
  • Every sector shows its job (the Roman numeral) and its name in the key you picked. Change the key and the names change; the wheel does not, which is the whole lesson: the shape is the song.
  • The hub shows the current chord big, with four beat lamps underneath so you can count the bar.
  • Tonic sectors are faintly filled, so even at a glance you can see how much of the orbit is home.
  • Drag the tempo slider and the wheel and the band stay locked. The ⛶ button goes fullscreen for the projector.

Why it is here

The 12 Blocks walked the blues down a street. This one puts the same form in orbit, because a chord progression is not a line, it is a loop: bar 12 does not end anything, it slings you around to bar 1. A wheel makes that impossible to miss. Watch the turnaround sectors come around the top and you will start hearing the V7♭9 before it arrives, which is the moment the form stops being a diagram and starts being something you can feel coming.

The look is deliberately spare: black, white, thin lines, a crosshair. When there is only one color, the only things left to design with are motion, weight, and rhythm. That constraint is a good exercise, and it might be yours in Week 6.

Tools used

  • HTML canvas for the drawing, one requestAnimationFrame loop
  • Web Audio API for the band: shuffle drums, walking bass with chromatic approach notes, seventh-chord comping, all synthesized live in any key
  • The animation clock is the audio clock, which is why nothing drifts
  • A tiny spring simulation gives the wheel its game-show click
  • No libraries, no build step, one file
