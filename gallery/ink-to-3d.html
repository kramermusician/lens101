Ink to 3D, your handwriting as a 3D model.

Canvas + WebGL · Browser · 2026 · Kramer Gibson

Open Ink to 3D ›

Opens in a new tab. Works on a laptop or straight from your phone's camera.

What it is

Write your name big on white paper and take a photo. The page finds the ink, lets you clean it up, and hands you a 3D model of your own handwriting. Drop it into Blender, and you can spin it, light it, and animate it.

Start here

  • Use a thick black marker. A thin pen works too, but you will want to push Stroke thickness up.
  • Shoot straight down in even light, with the paper filling most of the photo.
  • Blue is what becomes 3D. Paint to add what it missed, erase smudges, crop away the table.
  • Download the GLB. In Blender: File, Import, glTF 2.0. Your letters stand up, facing the front view.
  • Add a Bevel modifier for soft edges, then go animate.

GLB or OBJ?

Both are 3D models of the same shape. A GLB is one small file that also carries your name and the color you picked, so it opens in Blender looking the way it did in the preview. An OBJ is an older, plain-text format that holds only the shape, with no color. Almost every 3D app can open an OBJ, so it is the backup when something will not take a GLB. For Blender, pick GLB.

Your files

Nothing is uploaded. The photo stays in your browser, and the model downloads straight to your computer as a GLB (or an OBJ for other 3D apps).

Tools used

  • HTML canvas for reading the photo, marching squares for tracing the ink
  • WebGL for the spinning preview, a hand-written GLB and OBJ writer
  • One file, no libraries, no build step
