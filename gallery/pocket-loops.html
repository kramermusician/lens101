Pocket Loops, an animation tool that lives in your hand.

Canvas + camera · Browser · 2026 · Kramer Gibson

Open Pocket Loops ›

Made for a phone held in one hand. Works on a laptop too, with fewer tricks.

What it is

Flipbook Lab is a studio. This is a toy. Every coin at the bottom is a thread: its own short loop, in its own color, with its own number of frames, and all of them spin together at one tempo. You draw with your thumb while the loop plays, or pause and flick through the frames one at a time with the last frame ghosted in red and the next in green. A thread can also stand still, so the room stays put while the thing in it moves. Point the camera at something and steal its color.

Start here

  • Press play and watch the starter: a ball on six frames, a ground that stands still, a sun that drifts on three.
  • Tap the red coin, then draw. Your thread freezes while your finger is down and runs again when you lift it. Two fingers tap to undo.
  • Press pause. Flick left across the canvas to reach the next frame; the frame you just left shows in red. Draw the next step.
  • Add a thread and set it to Still. Draw the room. Now everything else moves through it.
  • Tap the line button and every touch becomes a straight line. Tap the camera, drag the ring over a color, snap it.
  • The ? at the top brings the help cards back any time.

Habits, not features

  • Put a 2-frame thread next to a 3-frame thread and watch where they meet. That is polyrhythm, and it is also animation.
  • Tap the tempo to a song. Draw something that agrees with it.
  • Press Spark in the menu when you are stuck. It hands you a constraint.

Your files

Nothing you make is uploaded anywhere. The page autosaves in your browser, Save gives you a small project file (name.loops.json), and Export hands you a GIF or a short video through your phone's share sheet.

Tools used

  • HTML canvas and pointer events for drawing, with pressure on a stylus and speed-sensitive width on a finger
  • getUserMedia for the camera; MediaRecorder for video; the Web Share API for handing files to Photos
  • One small library (gif.js) fetched only when you export a GIF
  • One file, no build step
