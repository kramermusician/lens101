Pocket Loops, an animation tool that lives in your hand.

Canvas + phone sensors · Browser · 2026 · Kramer Gibson

Open Pocket Loops ›

Made for a phone held in one hand. Works on a laptop too, with fewer tricks.

What it is

Flipbook Lab is a studio. This is a toy. Every coin at the bottom is a thread: its own short loop, in its own color, with its own number of frames, and all of them spin together at one tempo. You draw with your thumb while the loop plays. Then the phone itself joins in: tilt it to crank the loop by hand, talk to it and a thread follows your voice, shake it and every line starts to shiver, point the camera at something and steal its color.

Start here

  • Press play and watch the starter: a ball on six frames, a ground line on two, a sun that follows the tilt of the phone.
  • Tap the red coin, then draw. Your thread freezes while your finger is down and runs again when you lift it. Two fingers tap to undo.
  • Turn on the crank (the wheel button) and roll the phone left or right. Now draw one long line while you roll. Play it.
  • Add a thread with three frames. Draw a mouth closed, half open, open. Tap the coin again, set it to Voice, and say your name.
  • Shake the phone. Tap the wave to calm it down.

Habits, not features

  • Put a 2-frame thread next to a 3-frame thread and watch where they meet. That is polyrhythm, and it is also animation.
  • Tap the tempo to a song. Draw something that agrees with it.
  • Press Spark in the menu when you are stuck. It hands you a constraint.
  • Record ten seconds with the mic on. The sound goes into the video.

Your files

Nothing you make is uploaded anywhere. The page autosaves in your browser, Save gives you a small project file (name.loops.json), and Export hands you a GIF or a short video through your phone's share sheet.

Tools used

  • HTML canvas and pointer events for drawing, with pressure on a stylus and speed-sensitive width on a finger
  • DeviceOrientation and DeviceMotion for tilt, crank and shake; Web Audio for the voice thread; getUserMedia for the camera; MediaRecorder for video; the Web Share API for handing files to Photos
  • One small library (gif.js) fetched only when you export a GIF
  • One file, no build step
