The 12 Blocks, a walkable blues.

Canvas + Web Audio · Browser · 2026 · Kramer Gibson

How to watch

  • Click once to start. Sound on, this piece is half music.
  • One city block is one bar. The walker crosses a block every four beats.
  • Blue buildings are the tonic, gold is the four, red is the five. Same color, same job, every time.
  • The windows are the chord. Each row of windows is one chord tone, sitting at its real pitch height, labeled R, 3, 5, ♭7 up the left side. A minor chord's third floor is one story lower than a major chord's, the half-diminished drops its fifth too, and the whole voicing climbs as the root climbs, so the skyline is the harmony. Watch the walking bass light up the floors one at a time.
  • Tap MAJOR to switch to the minor blues. The five chord stays red, the flat six moves in down the street in deep blue portholes, and bar 12 splits into two skinny buildings: the iiø7 to V7 turnaround, two chords sharing one block.
  • Pick any key. The whole neighborhood transposes and the bass stays in a real bass register.
  • Pick a feel: shuffle, rock, halftime, reggae, or 12/8. In reggae the windows flash on the offbeat skank instead of the backbeat, which is the whole lesson in one glance.
  • The crosswalks are the barlines. The sign at block 9 says turnaround, because that is where the home stretch begins and the form starts pulling you back to the top.
  • Tap MAP to see the whole chorus at once, four bars to a line, the way a chart is written. The walker keeps strolling the grid so you always know where the band is inside the form.
  • Drag the tempo slider and notice the walk and the groove stay locked together. The ⛶ button goes fullscreen for the projector.

Why it is here

This is my take on the assignment I will give you in Week 6: make a visual map of the 12-bar blues in a tool of your choice. Twelve bars, three chords, one shape you can hear coming. I chose to make the form a neighborhood, because that is how the blues feels to me: you know every block, you know the one red building near the end, and the whole point is that the walk brings you home.

Yours does not need code, or sound, or a walker in a red beanie. It needs a decision about what the I, the IV, and the V look like, and it needs the turnaround to feel like a turn. Everything else is your voice.

Tools used

  • HTML canvas for the drawing, one requestAnimationFrame loop
  • Web Audio API for the band: bass, seventh-chord comping, kick, snare, hats, all synthesized live in five grooves and any key
  • The animation clock is the audio clock, which is why nothing drifts
  • No libraries, no build step, one file
